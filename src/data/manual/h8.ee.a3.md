## What it is

Very big and very small numbers are easier to handle as one digit times a power of ten. Once both numbers are written that way, comparing them is mostly comparing exponents.

**Key idea: round to one digit times a power of 10. Compare sizes by comparing exponents: 4 × 10⁸ is about 200 times 2 × 10⁶.**

## How to solve it

1. Find the first digit of the number. That is the digit you keep.
2. Drop everything after it. This is an estimate, and one digit is the whole point.
3. Count how many places the decimal point moves to sit just after that digit. Moving left gives a positive exponent; moving right gives a negative one.
4. Write it as one digit times that power of ten. To compare two of them, **divide** — subtracting tells you the gap, not how many times bigger.

## Watch out for

- **Mistake:** counting the zeros wrong, so 4,200,000 comes out as 4 × 10⁵. **Fix:** count the places the point moves, not the zeros you can see. 4,200,000 needs six moves, so 4 × 10⁶.
- **Mistake:** subtracting when the question asks how many times larger. **Fix:** "how many times" always means divide. 4 × 10⁸ ÷ 2 × 10⁶ is 2 × 10², about 200 times, not a difference.

## Where it shows up

- **Intercept sorties** — radar wavelengths, which are fractions of a metre.
- **Hangar** — aircraft range in metres and fleet costs.
- **Systems:** Radar, Avionics.
- **Real aircraft:** the SR-71 was designed to cruise above 85,000 ft, roughly 2.6 × 10⁴ m. The T-38 is 1.4 × 10¹ m long.
