## What it is

Angles that sit together follow rules. Name the relationship and it gives you an equation; solve the equation and you have the angle. The last step is the one people skip.

**Key idea: Complementary = 90, supplementary = 180, vertical angles are EQUAL. Name the relationship, write the equation, solve — then answer the ANGLE, not x.**

## How to solve it

1. Name the relationship first. Complementary angles add to 90; supplementary angles add to 180.
2. Vertical angles — the pair directly opposite each other where two lines cross — are EQUAL, not supplementary.
3. Write the equation the relationship gives you and solve it. With two expressions in x, add them and set the total.
4. Answer the ANGLE. Put x back into the expression the question actually asked about.

## Watch out for

- **Mistake:** using 90 where 180 belongs, or the other way round. **Fix:** complementary makes a corner, supplementary makes a straight line. Picture which one you are looking at.
- **Mistake:** solving for x and stopping there. **Fix:** x is a step, not the answer. If the question asked for an angle in degrees, put x back in.
- **Mistake:** treating vertical angles as supplementary. **Fix:** opposite angles at a crossing are equal. It is the ones NEXT to each other that add to 180.

## Where it shows up

- **Briefing** — where two flight paths cross.
- **Sortie** — intercept headings against the target's track.
- **Systems:** Avionics.
- **Real aircraft:** a runway numbered 09 at one end is 27 at the other, because the two headings are 180 apart.
