## What it is

Most square roots are not whole numbers, so you estimate them. **Bracketing** means trapping a root between the two perfect squares either side of it, then closing in.

**Key idea: bracket the root between two perfect squares (√40 is between 6 and 7, closer to 6). Estimate to the tenth, then place it on the number line.**

## How to solve it

1. List the perfect squares either side of the number under the root. For √40: 36 and 49, so 6² and 7².
2. Because 36 < 40 < 49, the root sits between 6 and 7. Notice which end it is closer to; 40 is much nearer 36, so the root is nearer 6.
3. Square a few tenths between them until you close in. 6.3² = 39.69 and 6.4² = 40.96, so √40 is just over 6.3.
4. Round to the place you were asked for, then put it on the number line beside anything else you are comparing.

## Watch out for

- **Mistake:** halving instead of rooting, so √16 comes out as 8. **Fix:** a square root asks what number times itself gives you this. 4 × 4 = 16, so √16 = 4. Halving and rooting only agree at 4.
- **Mistake:** bracketing between the wrong pair of squares, so √40 is called between 5 and 6. **Fix:** square your two guesses and check the number really falls between them. 5² = 25 and 6² = 36, and 40 is above both.

## Where it shows up

- **Intercept sorties** — the side of a square search box from its area.
- **Refuel and escort** — diagonal distances across a rectangular pattern.
- **Systems:** Radar, Weapons.
- **Real aircraft:** the T-38 is 14.0 m long and 7.6 m across. The diagonal of a box that size is √(14.0² + 7.6²), which brackets between 15 and 16 m.
