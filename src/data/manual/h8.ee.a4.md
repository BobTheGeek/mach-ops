## What it is

Scientific notation writes any number as a coefficient times a power of ten, with the coefficient always at least 1 and less than 10. That one rule is what makes two numbers comparable at a glance.

**Key idea: the coefficient must be at least 1 and less than 10. Multiply: multiply coefficients, add exponents, then fix the coefficient. Add: match the exponents first.**

## How to solve it

1. Move the decimal point so exactly one non-zero digit sits in front of it, and count the places you moved.
2. Write the count as the exponent. Left is positive, right is negative.
3. To multiply, multiply the coefficients and add the exponents. To divide, divide and subtract. **To add or subtract you must match the exponents first**, because you cannot add a number of thousands to a number of millions until they are the same unit.
4. Check the coefficient is still between 1 and 10. If it slipped to 12, shift the point and add one to the exponent.

## Watch out for

- **Mistake:** leaving a coefficient outside the range, so the answer reads 12 × 10⁵. **Fix:** 12 × 10⁵ is 1.2 × 10⁶. Shift the point one place left and add one to the exponent.
- **Mistake:** reversing the exponent sign for a small number, so 0.00042 comes out as 4.2 × 10⁴. **Fix:** small numbers get negative exponents. 0.00042 is 4.2 × 10⁻⁴.
- **Mistake:** adding the exponents when adding the numbers. **Fix:** adding exponents is the multiplication rule. To add, put both on the same power of ten and add only the coefficients.

## Where it shows up

- **Hangar** — aircraft range in metres, fuel mass in grams, fleet totals in credits.
- **Intercept sorties** — closure rates over long distances.
- **Systems:** Radar, Avionics, Engines.
- **Real aircraft:** the SR-71 is 32.73 m long, which is 3.273 × 10¹ m. Its design cruise altitude of 85,000 ft is about 2.59 × 10⁴ m.
