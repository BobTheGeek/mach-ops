## What it is

An **integer** is a whole number that can be positive, negative, or zero: 12,000 ft is an integer, and so is −3,500 ft (below sea level). Subtracting integers is how you find a change ("I was at 12,000 and now I'm at −3,500 — what happened?") or a distance ("how far apart are these two readings?").

**Key idea: subtracting a number is the same as adding its opposite.** 7 − (−3) means 7 + 3. Subtracting a negative moves you to the right on the number line, not the left.

## How to solve it

1. Write the subtraction. For a change, it is always **final − initial**.
2. Keep the first number. Change the − to +. Change the second number to its **opposite** (flip its sign). Some pilots call this *keep, change, change*.
3. Add using the integer rules: same signs, add the sizes and keep the sign; different signs, subtract the sizes and keep the sign of the larger one.
4. Check on the number line. Subtracting a positive moves left; subtracting a negative moves right. Did you move the right way?

For a **distance** between two readings, take the absolute value of the difference: distance = |a − b|. Distance is never negative.

## Watch out for

- **Mistake:** 7 − (−3) = 4. **Fix:** two negatives next to each other become a plus. 7 − (−3) = 7 + 3 = 10.
- **Mistake:** flipping the sign of the *first* number instead of the second. **Fix:** only the number being subtracted gets its opposite. The first number stays exactly as written.
- **Mistake:** computing b − a when the problem asked for a − b. **Fix:** subtraction is not commutative; order matters. Read which value is first.
- **Mistake:** change computed as initial − final. **Fix:** change is where you ended minus where you started. Descending from 12,000 to 4,000 is 4,000 − 12,000 = −8,000: a negative change.

## Where it shows up

- **Intercept sorties** — altitude changes when you follow a diving bogey.
- **Refuel and escort** — fuel margin above or below reserve, before and after a leg.
- **Systems:** Avionics (altitude), Fuel.
- **Real aircraft:** the SR-71 climbed to 85,000 ft while the outside air was around −56 °C. Going from ground temperature of 30 °C to −56 °C is a change of −56 − 30 = −86 °C.
