## What it is

A compound event is two or three things happening together: clear weather AND a radar lock. Counting the ways it can happen works exactly as before, once you have the whole sample space.

**Key idea: List every outcome of the combined event, then P = favourable ÷ total. For independent events, P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B).**

## How to solve it

1. Count the outcomes of each stage on its own. A tree grows one column per stage.
2. MULTIPLY the counts to size the whole sample space. Two stages of 3 and 4 give 12 outcomes, not 7.
3. For independent stages, P(all of them) is the probabilities MULTIPLIED together.
4. For "at least one", work out P(none of them) and take it from 1. That is far quicker than listing every way to get at least one.

## Watch out for

- **Mistake:** adding the probabilities, so P(A and B) becomes P(A) + P(B). **Fix:** adding makes the answer BIGGER, and "both" is always less likely than either one alone. 1/2 × 1/3 = 1/6, not 5/6.
- **Mistake:** sizing the sample space by adding the stages. **Fix:** every outcome of the first stage pairs with every outcome of the second, so the counts multiply.

## Where it shows up

- **Briefing** — clear weather AND a radar lock on the same pass.
- **Sortie** — two missiles both hitting.
- **Systems:** Weapons, Avionics.
- **Real aircraft:** a system with two independent backups fails only if both fail, which is why the combined figure is a product.
