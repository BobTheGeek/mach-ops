## What it is

Once a sample is random you can scale what you found in it up to the whole population. The estimate will not be exact, and a different sample would give a different one.

**Key idea: Estimate for the population = the sample's share × the population size. A sample MEAN is already an estimate of the population mean; you do not scale it up.**

## How to solve it

1. Work out the share the sample found: the count divided by the sample size.
2. Multiply that share by the WHOLE population. The sample count on its own is not an estimate of anything bigger.
3. For an average, stop after the sample mean. It is already a per-one figure and needs no scaling.
4. Expect samples to disagree. Several random samples give several estimates; quote the middle of them and say how wide they spread.

## Watch out for

- **Mistake:** expecting every sample to give the same answer, and calling the second one wrong. **Fix:** variation between samples is normal. It is why you take more than one.
- **Mistake:** reporting the sample count as the population figure. **Fix:** 9 duds in a sample of 60 is a share of 9/60, which over 2,400 missiles is 360 duds, not 9.

## Where it shows up

- **Hangar** — how many of the depot's missiles are duds, from a sample of sixty.
- **Debrief** — average sortie length across a wing.
- **Systems:** Weapons, Fuel.
- **Real aircraft:** fleet-wide defect rates are always estimates scaled up from inspections of a sample.
