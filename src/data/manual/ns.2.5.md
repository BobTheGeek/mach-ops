## What it is

Dividing rational numbers is one move: turn the division into a multiplication. A **complex fraction** — a fraction with a fraction on top or bottom — is not a new kind of problem, it is a division problem written differently.

**Key idea: decide the sign. Keep the first fraction, change division to multiplication, flip the second fraction. A complex fraction is just a division problem.**

## How to solve it

1. Decide the sign first. Same signs give a positive answer, different signs a negative one — the same rule as multiplication.
2. Write both numbers as improper fractions. If you are looking at a complex fraction, the top is the first number and the bottom is the second.
3. Keep the first, change the ÷ to ×, and flip the second upside down. The flipped fraction is called the **reciprocal**.
4. Multiply straight across and simplify.

## Watch out for

- **Mistake:** flipping the first fraction instead of the second. **Fix:** only the number you are dividing **by** gets flipped. The first one is kept exactly as it is.
- **Mistake:** flipping both fractions. **Fix:** flipping both undoes itself and gives the wrong answer. Keep, change, flip — one flip only.
- **Mistake:** getting the fractions right and the sign wrong. **Fix:** settle the sign in step 1 and write it down before you touch the numbers.

## Where it shows up

- **Refuel and escort** — how many legs a given fuel margin covers when each leg takes a fraction of a tank.
- **Intercept sorties** — a rate divided by a rate, which is where complex fractions turn up.
- **Systems:** Fuel, Engine.
- **Real aircraft:** the T-38 is 14.0 m long and 7.6 m across the wings. The span as a fraction of the length is 7.6 ÷ 14.0 = 0.545, the ratio checked against the USAF fact sheet.
