## What it is

A fraction and a decimal are two ways of writing the same number. The gauge shows 7/8 of a tank; the digital readout shows 0.875. Long division is how you get from one to the other.

**Key idea: divide numerator by denominator. The decimal terminates if the remainder hits 0, and repeats if a remainder repeats. Put a bar over the repeating digits.**

## How to solve it

1. Divide the numerator by the denominator. The numerator is the one that goes inside the division box.
2. Bring down a zero and keep dividing, writing each digit after the decimal point.
3. Watch the remainders. If one reaches 0 the decimal **terminates** — it ends. If a remainder you have already seen comes back, everything since then **repeats** forever.
4. Write the answer. Put a bar over the repeating digits, and only over those.

Going the other way, from a decimal to a fraction: read the last place value, write the digits over it, and reduce. 0.041 is 41 thousandths, so 41/1000.

## Watch out for

- **Mistake:** dividing the denominator by the numerator, so 7/8 comes out as 1.142857… **Fix:** the top number goes inside the box. 7/8 means 7 ÷ 8, which is less than 1.
- **Mistake:** putting the bar over the wrong digits, so 1/6 is written as 0.16 with a bar over both. **Fix:** only the digits that actually repeat get the bar. 1/6 = 0.1 then 6 forever, so the bar goes over the 6 alone.
- **Mistake:** writing 1/3 as 0.33 and calling it exact. **Fix:** 0.33 is a rounded value, not the number. Write 0.3 with a bar, or leave it as 1/3.

## Where it shows up

- **Refuel and escort** — the mechanical fuel gauge reads in fractions, the digital readout in decimals, and they must agree.
- **Intercept sorties** — Mach numbers written as fractions of Mach 1.
- **Systems:** Fuel, Avionics.
- **Real aircraft:** the T-38 is listed at Mach 1.08 at sea level. As a fraction of Mach 1 that is 27/25, and 27 ÷ 25 = 1.08 exactly, because 25 is only 5s.
