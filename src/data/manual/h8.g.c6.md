## What it is

Round solids have their own volume formulas, and they are all related: a cone is a third of its cylinder, and a sphere sits between the two.

**Key idea: Cylinder V = πr²h. Cone = one THIRD of the cylinder with the same base and height. Sphere V = (4/3)πr³.**

## How to solve it

1. Get the RADIUS first. Using the diameter as the radius makes a sphere EIGHT times too big.
2. A cylinder is πr²h: the circle's area times how tall it stands.
3. A cone is a THIRD of the cylinder with the same base and height. Do not leave the third out.
4. A sphere is (4/3)πr³ — CUBED, not squared. A half sphere is half of that.

## Watch out for

- **Mistake:** leaving out the one third for a cone. **Fix:** three cones fill the cylinder around them. Divide by 3.
- **Mistake:** squaring the radius for a sphere. **Fix:** a sphere is a solid in all three directions, so it is r³. If your units are not cubed, you have squared it.
- **Mistake:** using the diameter as the radius. **Fix:** halve it first. For a sphere the error is 2³ = 8 times over.

## Where it shows up

- **Hangar** — the capacity of an external fuel tank.
- **Briefing** — the volume of a nose cone or a radome.
- **Systems:** Fuel, Weapons.
- **Real aircraft:** a drop tank is a cylinder with a cone on each end, and its quoted capacity is those three volumes added.
