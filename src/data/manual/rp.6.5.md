## What it is

A discount takes a percent off a price. A markup adds a percent on. The fast way to do either is one multiplication, because a 25% discount means you still pay 75%.

**Key idea: sale price = price × (1 − discount). Selling price = cost × (1 + markup).**

## How to solve it

1. Decide whether the price is going down (discount) or up (markup).
2. Build the multiplier. Down 25% gives 1 − 0.25 = 0.75. Up 30% gives 1 + 0.30 = 1.30.
3. Multiply the price by that multiplier once. That is the final price, not the saving.
4. For stacked discounts, multiply the multipliers together. 20% then 10% is 0.80 × 0.90 = 0.72, which is 28% off, not 30%.

## Watch out for

- **Mistake:** giving the discount amount as the sale price. **Fix:** $10 off a $40 jacket means you pay $30, not $10. Subtract, or use the 0.75 shortcut.
- **Mistake:** adding stacked percents, so 20% then 10% becomes 30% off. **Fix:** the second discount comes off an already smaller price. Multiply the multipliers: 0.80 × 0.90 = 0.72.
- **Mistake:** subtracting a markup. **Fix:** a markup makes the price bigger. The multiplier is over 1.

- **Hangar** — base shop sales and stacked coupons for paint schemes.
