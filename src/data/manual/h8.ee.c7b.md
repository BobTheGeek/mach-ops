## What it is

When x appears on both sides, you gather it on one side first, then finish the way you always do. A hanger diagram is a good picture: whatever you take off one side you take off the other, or it tips.

**Key idea: simplify each side, move the variable terms to one side and the constants to the other, divide, then check.**

## How to solve it

1. Simplify each side. Distribute across any brackets — every term inside, not just the first — and combine like terms.
2. Move the variable terms to one side by doing the same thing to both sides. A term that crosses the equals sign comes out with its sign changed.
3. Move the constants to the other side the same way.
4. Divide both sides by the coefficient, then check by putting your answer back into the original equation.

## Watch out for

- **Mistake:** moving a term across the equals sign without changing its sign. **Fix:** you are not moving it, you are subtracting it from both sides. 3x + 5 = 8 becomes 3x = 8 − 5, not 8 + 5.
- **Mistake:** distributing to the first term only, so 4(5x + 3) comes out as 20x + 3. **Fix:** the 4 multiplies everything inside the brackets: 20x + 12.

## Where it shows up

- **Briefing** — the point where two loadouts cost the same.
- **Intercept sorties** — when a faster aircraft starting behind catches one ahead.
- **Systems:** Engines, Avionics.
- **Real aircraft:** the T-38 climbs at about 33,600 ft per minute at sea level. Working out when a slower aircraft with a head start is overtaken is exactly this equation.
