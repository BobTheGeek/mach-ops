## What it is

Two points on a grid have a right triangle hiding between them. Draw it, and the distance between the points is just the hypotenuse.

**Key idea: Draw the right triangle between the points: the legs are the differences in x and in y. Then a² + b² = c².**

## How to solve it

1. Draw the right triangle: go across from the first point, then up to the second. The two points are the ends of the sloping side.
2. The legs are the DIFFERENCES: how far across in x, and how far up in y. Take them as distances, so never negative.
3. Watch the negatives. 3 − (−4) is 7, not −1: subtracting a negative moves you further apart, not closer.
4. Now it is Pythagoras: square the legs, add, take the square root.

## Watch out for

- **Mistake:** reporting one of the legs as the distance. **Fix:** the across and the up are two sides of the triangle. The distance is the third one, and it is longer than either.
- **Mistake:** getting the sign wrong subtracting negative coordinates. **Fix:** count the squares on the grid instead. From −4 to 3 is 7 steps, whichever way you write the subtraction.

## Where it shows up

- **Sortie HUD** — the distance between two radar contacts on the tactical grid.
- **Briefing** — leg lengths between waypoints.
- **Systems:** Avionics.
- **Real aircraft:** a moving map computes range to a waypoint exactly this way, from the difference in coordinates.
