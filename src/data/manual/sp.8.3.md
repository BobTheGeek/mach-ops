## What it is

Two groups almost always have different means. The question is whether the difference is big compared with how spread out each group already is.

**Key idea: Compare centres AND spreads. Divide the difference in centres by the MAD to see how big the difference really is. Two MADs or more is a difference worth reporting.**

## How to solve it

1. Find the centre of each group, so you have one number for each.
2. Subtract to get the gap between the centres, and say which way round it goes.
3. Find the spread. The MAD says how far a typical value sits from its own centre.
4. Divide the gap by the MAD. Half a MAD is nothing; two or three MADs is a real difference.

## Watch out for

- **Mistake:** comparing centres and stopping there. **Fix:** a gap of 3 minutes means a lot when the MAD is 1 and almost nothing when the MAD is 6.
- **Mistake:** quoting the MAD as if it were the gap. **Fix:** the MAD is a spread. The gap is the difference between the two centres. They are different numbers doing different jobs.

## Where it shows up

- **Debrief** — Eagle squadron's sortie times against Falcon's.
- **Hangar** — fuel burn on two different airframes.
- **Systems:** Fuel, Engines.
- **Real aircraft:** comparing two training syllabuses means comparing both the average result and how much the results vary.
