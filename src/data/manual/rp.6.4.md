## What it is

Percent change tells you how big a change was compared with where you started. Percent error does the same thing for a guess compared with the truth. Both divide by the number you started with, never by the one you ended with.

**Key idea: percent change = (new − original) ÷ ORIGINAL × 100. Percent error = |estimate − actual| ÷ actual × 100.**

## How to solve it

1. Name the original value. For percent change it is the old value; for percent error it is the ACTUAL value, not your estimate.
2. Subtract to get the difference. For percent error take the size of the difference, so it is never negative.
3. Divide the difference by the ORIGINAL. That is the whole trick: the bottom is always where you started.
4. Multiply by 100 and add the % sign. Say whether it went up or down.

## Watch out for

- **Mistake:** dividing by the new value. **Fix:** the original is the thing you are comparing against, so it goes on the bottom.
- **Mistake:** giving the raw difference as if it were a percent, so a rise from 40 to 50 is called "10%". **Fix:** 10 out of 40 is 25%. Divide before you label it.
- **Mistake:** thinking up 50% then down 50% comes back to the start. **Fix:** 100 up 50% is 150; 50% of 150 is 75, so you land on 75. The second percent is taken of a bigger number.

## Where it shows up

- **Debrief** — top speed gained after an engine upgrade.
- **Briefing** — your fuel estimate against what you actually burnt.
- **Systems:** Engines, Fuel.
- **Real aircraft:** the T-38C upgrade raised the avionics standard; comparing old and new figures is a percent change every time.
