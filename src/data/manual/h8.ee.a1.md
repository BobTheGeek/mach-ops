## What it is

An exponent counts how many times a base is multiplied by itself. The rules below are all shortcuts for writing that out and cancelling.

**Key idea: same base, multiplying means add the exponents. Dividing means subtract. A power of a power means multiply. Anything to the 0 is 1. A negative exponent means 1 over the positive power.**

## How to solve it

1. Check that the parts share the same base. The rules only work when they do; 3² × 5² does not simplify to a single power.
2. Apply the rule for what is being done. Multiplying adds the exponents, dividing subtracts them, and a power of a power multiplies them.
3. Read what the exponent came to. Zero means the whole thing is 1. A negative exponent means one over the positive power, not a negative answer.
4. Write the result as a single power. The base never changes.

## Watch out for

- **Mistake:** multiplying the exponents when the bases are being multiplied, so 3² × 3⁴ comes out as 3⁸. **Fix:** write it out. 3² × 3⁴ is (3×3) × (3×3×3×3), which is six 3s: 3⁶. Multiplying adds.
- **Mistake:** multiplying the bases as well, so 3² × 3⁴ comes out as 9⁶. **Fix:** the base is what is being multiplied; it stays as it is. Only the count of them changes.
- **Mistake:** reading 2⁻³ as −8. **Fix:** a negative exponent is about division, not sign. 2⁻³ = 1/2³ = 1/8, which is positive.
- **Mistake:** reading 5⁰ as 0. **Fix:** 5⁰ = 5¹ ÷ 5¹ = 1. Anything except zero to the power 0 is 1.

## Where it shows up

- **Intercept sorties** — signal strength halving with distance, which is 2⁻ⁿ.
- **Refuel and escort** — squadron numbers doubling.
- **Systems:** Radar, Engines.
- **Real aircraft:** radar range figures are usually quoted as powers of ten, which is the same rule with a base of 10.
