## What it is

On a proportional graph the slope IS the unit rate. That means a graph, a table, an equation and a sentence can all be compared, once you pull the same number out of each.

**Key idea: On a proportional graph the slope is the unit rate, and the line passes through (0, 0). Compare two relationships by comparing SLOPES.**

## How to solve it

1. Check the line goes through (0, 0). A proportional relationship always does.
2. Slope is RISE over RUN: the change in y divided by the change in x. Upside down gives a different rate entirely.
3. To compare two of them, work out both rates as numbers first.
4. The point (1, r) says what happens in ONE unit, and (0, 0) says nothing has happened yet.

## Watch out for

- **Mistake:** dividing run by rise, so a rate of 3 comes out as 1/3. **Fix:** rise is on TOP. Say the units: kilometres per minute means km on top.
- **Mistake:** judging by which line looks steeper. **Fix:** two graphs can use different scales. Work out both numbers before you compare them.

## Where it shows up

- **Briefing** — two tankers' fuel-burn graphs side by side.
- **Debrief** — speed from a table against speed from a graph.
- **Systems:** Fuel, Engines.
- **Real aircraft:** a climb-rate chart is read exactly this way, and two aircraft's charts rarely share a scale.
