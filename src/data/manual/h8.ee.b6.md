## What it is

Any straight line can be written as y = mx + b. m is how steep it is, b is where it crosses the y-axis, and two points are enough to find both.

**Key idea: m = rise ÷ run = (y2 − y1) ÷ (x2 − x1), the same between ANY two points on the line. b is where it crosses the y-axis.**

## How to solve it

1. Slope is RISE over RUN: the change in y divided by the change in x, in that order.
2. The slope is the same between any two points on the line, which is why one pair is enough.
3. b is where the line crosses the Y-axis. Work it out from a point you have: b = y − mx.
4. Watch the sign. A line falling from left to right has a NEGATIVE slope.

## Watch out for

- **Mistake:** dividing run by rise. **Fix:** rise over run. Going across 2 and up 6 is a slope of 3, not 1/3.
- **Mistake:** reading b off the x-axis. **Fix:** b is the Y-intercept, where x is 0. The x-intercept is a different point.
- **Mistake:** losing the minus on a falling line. **Fix:** if y goes DOWN as x goes up, m is negative. Check the picture against your number.

- **Briefing** — fuel remaining against time.
