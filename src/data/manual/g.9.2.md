## What it is

The area of a circle is how much ground it covers, not how far it is round the edge. It depends on the radius SQUARED, which is why doubling a radar's range covers four times the ground.

**Key idea: A = π × r². Square the radius FIRST, then multiply by π. If you were given the diameter, halve it before you start.**

## How to solve it

1. Get the RADIUS. If the question gave you the diameter, halve it before you do anything else.
2. Square the radius, then multiply by π. Squaring the whole of πr is a different and much bigger number.
3. A half circle is half of that — halve at the END, not the radius at the start.
4. Going backwards from an area, divide by π first and then take the square root.

## Watch out for

- **Mistake:** using the diameter in place of the radius, which makes the answer four times too big. **Fix:** halve first. A diameter of 20 means a radius of 10, and the area is 100π, not 400π.
- **Mistake:** computing (πr)² instead of πr². **Fix:** only the r is squared. π stays outside, multiplied once.
- **Mistake:** forgetting to square at all, so πr² comes out as πr. **Fix:** that is the circumference over 2. An area must have squared units.

## Where it shows up

- **Sortie HUD** — how much ground a radar sweep covers.
- **Briefing** — the area a flare lights up.
- **Systems:** Avionics, Weapons.
- **Real aircraft:** doubling a radar's range multiplies the area it watches by four, which is why range matters more than it looks.
