## What it is

Cut a solid straight through and look at the cut face. That flat shape is the cross-section, and which shape it is depends on the solid AND on the direction of the cut.

**Key idea: A slice PARALLEL to the base is a copy of the base. A slice straight DOWN through a prism or cylinder is a rectangle; through a pyramid or cone it is a triangle.**

## How to solve it

1. Picture the cut face on its own, as if you lifted the top half off and looked straight down at it.
2. A slice PARALLEL to the base is a copy of the base: a cylinder gives a circle, a triangular prism gives a triangle.
3. A slice straight DOWN through a prism or a cylinder is a rectangle, whatever its base looks like.
4. A pyramid or cone narrows to a point, so a slice straight down through the tip is a triangle. Parallel to the base it is still a smaller copy of the base.

## Watch out for

- **Mistake:** expecting every slice of a pyramid to be a triangle. **Fix:** slice a square pyramid parallel to its base and you get a SQUARE — just a smaller one.
- **Mistake:** expecting every slice of a cylinder to be a circle. **Fix:** slice it straight down and you get a rectangle, as tall as the cylinder and as wide as its diameter.

## Where it shows up

- **Briefing** — fuselage and wing cross-sections on a spec sheet.
- **Hangar** — the baffles inside a fuel tank.
- **Systems:** Fuel, Engines.
- **Real aircraft:** an aircraft's cross-section drawings are exactly this: the shape you would see if you cut it through at one station.
