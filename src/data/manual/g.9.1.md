## What it is

Every circle has the same relationship between the distance round it and the distance across it. That relationship is π, and it is the only thing you need to turn one into the other.

**Key idea: Diameter = 2 × radius. C = π × d, which is the same as 2 × π × r. Circumference ÷ diameter is always π.**

## How to solve it

1. Sort out the radius and the diameter first. The diameter is twice the radius, so halve it if that is what you were given.
2. Circumference is π × diameter, or 2 × π × radius — the same thing said two ways. It is NOT π × radius.
3. Decide which form the question wants. "In terms of π" means you stop at 12π. "Use π = 3.14" means you multiply it out.
4. Going backwards from a circumference, divide by π to get the diameter, then halve it again for the radius.

## Watch out for

- **Mistake:** using C = πr, which gives half the right answer. **Fix:** the formula uses the DIAMETER. With the radius you need the 2 as well: C = 2πr.
- **Mistake:** reaching for πr², which is the AREA formula. **Fix:** circumference is a length, area is a covering. If the units come out squared, you did the wrong one.
- **Mistake:** putting the diameter where the radius belongs, or the other way round. **Fix:** label which one you were given before you write any formula down.

## Where it shows up

- **Briefing** — how far you fly round one lap of a holding pattern.
- **Sortie HUD** — turn radius against distance covered.
- **Systems:** Avionics, Fuel.
- **Real aircraft:** a standard rate turn takes two minutes for a full circle, so its circumference is your speed × two minutes.
