## What it is

A probability says how likely something is, as a number between 0 and 1. The sample space is the list of everything that could happen; the probability of an event is the share of that list which makes the event happen.

**Key idea: P(event) = favourable outcomes ÷ total outcomes, always between 0 and 1. 0 is impossible, 1 is certain, 1/2 is even either way.**

## How to solve it

1. Count the TOTAL number of outcomes. That means every item that could come out, not every kind of item: a bag of 9 live and 6 dud rounds has 15 outcomes, not 2.
2. Count the FAVOURABLE outcomes: the ones that make the event happen.
3. Write favourable over total and reduce it. The answer can never be more than 1.
4. Check it against the line. Zero is impossible, 1 is certain, 1/2 is even. For "NOT the event", take your answer away from 1.

## Watch out for

- **Mistake:** writing favourable over UNfavourable, so 9 live out of 15 becomes 9/6. **Fix:** that is more than 1, and no probability is. The bottom of the fraction is the total, every time.
- **Mistake:** assuming the kinds of outcome are equally likely, so a bag with three colours gives 1/3 for each. **Fix:** count the items, not the colours. Nine of one and one of another is 9/10 and 1/10.
- **Mistake:** giving P(A) when P(NOT A) was asked for. **Fix:** they add to 1. Work out one and subtract.

## Where it shows up

- **Briefing** — the chance a round in the bag is a dud.
- **Sortie HUD** — the chance of a radar contact on a given sweep.
- **Systems:** Weapons, Avionics.
- **Real aircraft:** a two-seat T-38 with one seat filled at random gives P(front seat) = 1/2.
