## What it is

Mean and median both claim to be the centre, and on skewed data they disagree. The shape of the data decides which one you should quote.

**Key idea: Symmetric with no outliers: use the mean and the MAD. Skewed, or with an outlier: use the median and the IQR.**

## How to solve it

1. Look at the shape. A mirror-image pile is symmetric. A long tail on one side means skewed towards that side.
2. Compare the mean with the median. A tail to the RIGHT drags the mean ABOVE the median; a tail to the left drags it below.
3. Hunt for outliers. One value far from the rest is enough to make the mean a poor summary on its own.
4. Choose. Symmetric and clean: mean and MAD. Skewed or an outlier present: median and IQR.

## Watch out for

- **Mistake:** reaching for the mean every time. **Fix:** one 240-minute abort among ten 50-minute sorties drags the mean up to 69 while the median stays at 50. The median is the honest one there.
- **Mistake:** naming the skew backwards. **Fix:** the skew is named for where the TAIL points, not where the pile sits. A pile on the left with a tail stretching right is skewed RIGHT.

## Where it shows up

- **Debrief** — sortie durations when one mission aborted.
- **Hangar** — kill counts when one pilot is an ace.
- **Systems:** Avionics, Engines.
- **Real aircraft:** aircraft ages in a mixed fleet are usually skewed, so a squadron quotes a median age, not a mean one.
