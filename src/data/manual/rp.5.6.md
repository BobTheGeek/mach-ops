## What it is

A scale drawing is the real thing shrunk or grown by a fixed factor. The scale tells you that factor: 1 cm : 25 km means every centimetre on the map is 25 km on the ground.

**Key idea: a scale is a ratio (drawing : actual). Multiply a drawing length by the scale factor to get the real length. Areas scale by the factor SQUARED.**

## How to solve it

1. Read the scale as drawing to actual. The number on the right is the scale factor.
2. Going from the drawing to the real thing, multiply. Going from real back to the drawing, divide.
3. For an **area**, multiply by the factor twice, because both sides of the shape grow. A 1 : 72 model has 1/72 the length but 1/5184 the area.
4. Write the answer with its units, and sanity-check the size.

## Watch out for

- **Mistake:** scaling an area by the factor once. **Fix:** area is length times width, and both scale, so the factor is used twice.
- **Mistake:** dividing when you should multiply. **Fix:** ask whether the answer should be bigger or smaller than what you started with.
- **Mistake:** adding the scale instead of multiplying by it. **Fix:** a scale is a ratio. 4 cm at 1 cm : 25 km is 100 km, not 29.

## Where it shows up

- **Briefing** — distances measured off the tactical map.
- **Hangar** — 1 : 72 model kits and blueprint dimensions.
- **Systems:** Avionics, Weapons.
- **Real aircraft:** the T-38 is 14.0 m long. At 1 : 72 a model is 19.4 cm; at 1 : 48 it is 29.2 cm.
