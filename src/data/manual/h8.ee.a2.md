## What it is

A square root undoes squaring; a cube root undoes cubing. √p is the side of a square with area p, and ∛p is the edge of a cube with volume p.

**Key idea: √p asks what number times ITSELF gives p. ∛p asks what number times itself THREE times gives p. Solve x² = p and x³ = p with roots.**

## How to solve it

1. Read the symbol. √ asks for a number times itself; ∛ asks for a number times itself three times.
2. It is not halving or thirding. √16 is 4, because 4 × 4 = 16.
3. Work up through the ones worth knowing: 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100 ... and 1, 8, 27, 64, 125, 216 ...
4. For a fraction, root the top and the bottom separately. √(1/4) is 1/2, because 1/2 × 1/2 = 1/4.

## Watch out for

- **Mistake:** halving instead of rooting, so √16 comes out as 8. **Fix:** check it. 8 × 8 is 64, not 16. The answer is 4.
- **Mistake:** dividing by three for a cube root, so ∛27 comes out as 9. **Fix:** 9 × 9 × 9 is 729. The answer is 3.
- **Mistake:** rooting a sum term by term, so √(9 + 16) becomes 3 + 4. **Fix:** add first. √25 is 5, not 7.

## Where it shows up

- **Hangar** — the side of a square landing pad from its area.
- **Briefing** — the edge of a cubic fuel cell from its volume.
- **Systems:** Fuel.
- **Real aircraft:** working back from a wing area to a representative chord is a square-root problem.
