## What it is

Simple interest is what borrowing or saving costs per year, worked out on the starting amount only. Three things go in: the principal P, the rate r, and the time t.

**Key idea: I = P × r × t, with r as a decimal and t in YEARS. Balance = P + I.**

## How to solve it

1. Write down P, r and t before you multiply anything.
2. Turn the rate into a decimal: 5% becomes 0.05.
3. Put the time in YEARS. 18 months is 1.5 years, not 18. 6 months is 0.5.
4. Multiply all three to get the interest. If the question asks for the balance, add the interest to the principal.

## Watch out for

- **Mistake:** using 5 instead of 0.05. **Fix:** the answer comes out 100 times too big. Interest on $400 at 5% for a year is $20, not $2,000.
- **Mistake:** putting 18 in for 18 months. **Fix:** the formula counts years. Divide months by 12 first.
- **Mistake:** giving the interest when the balance was asked for. **Fix:** the balance is the money you started with PLUS the interest. Read the last line of the question again.

## Where it shows up

- **Hangar** — a loan against the squadron credit account for a new airframe.
- **Debrief** — interest earned on banked mission credits.
- **Systems:** Credits ledger.
- **Real aircraft:** a flying club financing an airframe over three years pays interest on the amount borrowed, worked out exactly this way.
