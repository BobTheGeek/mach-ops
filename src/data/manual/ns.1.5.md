## What it is

Subtracting rational numbers is subtracting integers with fractions and decimals in the mix. You use the same first move every time — turn the subtraction into an addition — and then you are back to work you have already done in 1.3.

**Key idea: rewrite as adding the opposite, then use common denominators or aligned decimals. Distance on a number line is |a − b|.**

## How to solve it

1. Write the subtraction with both signs shown, the second number in its own brackets. Do not change anything yet.
2. Rewrite it as adding the **opposite**: keep the first number, change the − to +, and flip the sign of the second number. Some pilots call this *keep, change, change*.
3. Put both numbers in a matching form — a common denominator for fractions, lined-up decimal points for decimals.
4. Add using the sign rules, then simplify. If the question asked for a **distance**, take the absolute value at the end, because a distance is never negative.

## Watch out for

- **Mistake:** treating a − (−b) as if it were a − b, so 2.5 − (−1.5) comes out as 1.0. **Fix:** two minus signs next to each other become a plus. 2.5 − (−1.5) = 2.5 + 1.5 = 4.0.
- **Mistake:** subtracting the denominators as well as the numerators, so 3/4 − 1/2 comes out as 2/2. **Fix:** the denominator names the size of the pieces and never gets subtracted. Rewrite over a common denominator first: 3/4 − 2/4 = 1/4.
- **Mistake:** reporting a distance as a negative number. **Fix:** a distance is how far apart two readings are, so it is always zero or more. Take the absolute value of the difference: |a − b|.

## Where it shows up

- **Intercept sorties** — vertical separation between you and a contact below you, read off the vertical tape.
- **Refuel and escort** — how far a tank sits below its minimum, in fractions of a load.
- **Systems:** Avionics (vertical tape), Fuel, Environmental (skin and outside-air temperature).
- **Real aircraft:** the U-2 and the SR-71 both flew above 70,000 ft while a surface contact sat at sea level or below. The vertical gap between a target at −0.5 KFT and an aircraft at 70.0 KFT is |70.0 − (−0.5)| = 70.5 KFT.
