## What it is

A box plot squeezes a whole data set into five numbers and draws them to scale. Two of them side by side let you compare centre and spread at a glance.

**Key idea: Five numbers: minimum, Q1, median, Q3, maximum. The box is the middle 50%. Each of the four sections holds about a QUARTER of the data, however long it looks.**

## How to solve it

1. Name the five numbers in order: minimum, Q1, median, Q3, maximum. The plot draws them left to right.
2. The box runs from Q1 to Q3, so its width is the IQR — the middle half of the data.
3. The line inside the box is the median. It sits where the data puts it, not in the middle of the box.
4. Each of the four sections — lower whisker, lower box, upper box, upper whisker — holds about 25% of the values. A long section is spread out, not crowded.

## Watch out for

- **Mistake:** reading a long whisker as "more data over there". **Fix:** long means spread out. Each section still holds about a quarter of the values.
- **Mistake:** treating the box as half the VALUES rather than half the DATA, so the median is read as the middle of the range. **Fix:** the median splits the count in two, not the number line.

## Where it shows up

- **Debrief** — two squadrons' sortie times on one axis.
- **Hangar** — how lock times are distributed across a unit.
- **Systems:** Avionics, Fuel.
- **Real aircraft:** maintenance turnaround times are usually reported as box plots, because the tail matters as much as the middle.
