## What it is

A ratio pairs two quantities: 3 litres for every 2 nautical miles. A ratio table lists pairs that all mean the same rate, which is what makes it useful for finding one you do not know yet.

**Key idea: equivalent ratios come from multiplying or dividing BOTH quantities by the same number. Never add the same number to both.**

## How to solve it

1. Find a row where you know both values. That pair is your base ratio.
2. Work out what the known value was multiplied by to reach the row you want.
3. Multiply the OTHER value by that same number. Both parts scale together, or you have changed the ratio into a different one.
4. Write the missing value, and check it against another row if there is one.

## Watch out for

- **Mistake:** adding the same amount to both parts, so 3 : 2 becomes 5 : 4. **Fix:** 3 : 2 and 5 : 4 are different rates. Check by dividing: 3 ÷ 2 is 1.5, but 5 ÷ 4 is 1.25.
- **Mistake:** swapping the two quantities, so litres-per-mile becomes miles-per-litre. **Fix:** keep the labels on the rows. The row you multiply is the row you started from.

## Where it shows up

- **Briefing** — fuel needed for a leg from a known fuel-to-distance table.
- **Hangar** — missiles per sortie, pilots per aircraft.
- **Systems:** Fuel, Weapons, Avionics.
- **Real aircraft:** the T-38 carries two crew. Two crew per aircraft means eight crew for four aircraft, not four crew for four aircraft.
