## What it is

Real shapes are not squares. A composite figure is one you cut into pieces you already know — rectangles, triangles, half circles — work out each piece, and put them back together.

**Key idea: Cut the figure into shapes you know, then ADD (or SUBTRACT a piece that has been cut out). Perimeter counts only the OUTSIDE edges.**

## How to solve it

1. Cut the figure into shapes you know. Draw the cut lines in so you can see them.
2. Work out each piece on its own, then ADD them — or SUBTRACT the piece that has been taken away.
3. A half circle is half a circle. Halve the area, and halve the circumference for the curved edge.
4. For a perimeter, walk round the OUTSIDE. The lines you drew to cut the figure up are not part of it.

## Watch out for

- **Mistake:** counting the cut lines in the perimeter. **Fix:** walk your finger round the outside edge. If your finger never touches a line, it does not count.
- **Mistake:** using a whole circle where a half circle is drawn. **Fix:** halve it. Both the area and the curved edge are half of the full circle's.
- **Mistake:** adding the cut-out piece instead of taking it away. **Fix:** a hole makes the area SMALLER. Subtract.

## Where it shows up

- **Briefing** — runway and apron layouts.
- **Hangar** — floor area with a service pit cut out.
- **Systems:** Avionics, Fuel.
- **Real aircraft:** a hangar floor is almost never a rectangle, so the paint estimate is always a composite-area sum.
