## What it is

A rate compares two quantities with different units. A **unit rate** is the special one where the second quantity is 1: litres per minute, kilometres per litre. It is the number that lets you compare two things fairly.

**Key idea: a unit rate is 'per 1'. Divide the first quantity by the second. With fractions, divide by multiplying by the reciprocal. Label the units.**

## How to solve it

1. Decide which quantity you want ONE of. That one goes on the bottom of the division.
2. Divide the first quantity by the second.
3. If either quantity is a fraction, dividing means multiplying by the reciprocal: keep, change, flip.
4. Write the answer with its units. The units are how you check you divided the right way round.

## Watch out for

- **Mistake:** dividing the wrong way, so litres per minute comes out as minutes per litre. **Fix:** say the units out loud. "Per minute" means minutes go on the bottom.
- **Mistake:** multiplying when you should divide by a fraction, so (1/2) ÷ (1/4) comes out as 1/8. **Fix:** flip the second fraction and multiply: 1/2 × 4/1 = 2.
- **Mistake:** answering in the wrong unit. **Fix:** convert the units before you divide, not after, and keep the unit written on every line.

## Where it shows up

- **Refuel and escort** — fuel burn per minute, kilometres per litre.
- **Intercept sorties** — closure rate per minute, Mach per throttle percent.
- **Systems:** Fuel, Engines.
- **Real aircraft:** the T-38 is listed at 812 mph. Over half an hour that is 406 miles, and the unit rate is what makes that one step.
