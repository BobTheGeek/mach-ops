## What it is

A **rational number** is any number you can write as a fraction of two whole numbers, so fractions and decimals count as well as whole numbers. Adding them uses the same sign rules you already know from integers — the only new part is getting the two numbers into a form you can actually add.

**Key idea: apply the integer sign rules, then add like you already know: common denominators for fractions, line up the decimal points for decimals.**

## How to solve it

1. Write the addition with every sign shown, each number in its own brackets. Nothing has been changed yet; you are just laying it out so no sign hides.
2. Put the numbers in a matching form. For fractions, rewrite both over a **common denominator** — a denominator they both divide into. For decimals, line up the decimal points and give every number the same number of places.
3. Apply the sign rules to the sizes. Same signs, add the sizes and keep the sign. Different signs, subtract the smaller size from the bigger and keep the sign of the bigger one.
4. Add, then simplify. Reduce the fraction, or drop a trailing zero from a decimal, and write the units back on.

## Watch out for

- **Mistake:** adding the numerators and the denominators, so 1/2 + 1/3 comes out as 2/5. **Fix:** only the numerators get added, and only once both fractions are over the same denominator. 1/2 + 1/3 = 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6.
- **Mistake:** reading −2 1/4 as −2 plus 1/4. **Fix:** the minus sign belongs to the whole quantity. −2 1/4 means −(2 + 1/4), which is −9/4, not −7/4.
- **Mistake:** getting the fractions right but carrying an integer sign-rule slip into the answer. **Fix:** the sign rules do not change just because there are fractions. Settle the sign first, then do the arithmetic on the sizes.

## Where it shows up

- **Refuel and escort** — fuel tanks that read in fractions of a load, and fuel flow that reads in tenths of a litre per minute.
- **Intercept sorties** — altitude tapes that read in thousands of feet with a decimal, like 24.5 KFT.
- **Systems:** Fuel, Engine, Avionics (altitude tape).
- **Real aircraft:** the F-16C carries about 3.2 tonnes of internal fuel. Dropping 1.4 tonnes on the outbound leg and taking 0.9 tonnes from a tanker nets out as −1.4 + 0.9 = −0.5 tonnes against where you started.
