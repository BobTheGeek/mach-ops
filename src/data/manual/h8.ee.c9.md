## What it is

An inequality is not one line, it is a whole half of the plane. The line is the boundary, and the shading says which side counts.

**Key idea: Graph the boundary line (DASHED for < or >, SOLID for ≤ or ≥), test a point off it, and shade the side that makes the inequality true.**

## How to solve it

1. Draw the boundary line first, as if the inequality were an equals sign.
2. Pick the line style. DASHED for < and >, because those points are not included. SOLID for ≤ and ≥, because they are.
3. Test a point that is not on the line — (0, 0) if the line misses it — and see whether it makes the inequality true.
4. Shade the side the true point is on. With y on its own, > shades ABOVE and < shades BELOW.

## Watch out for

- **Mistake:** shading the wrong side. **Fix:** test a point. If it makes the inequality true, its side is the one to shade; if not, shade the other.
- **Mistake:** drawing a solid line for a strict inequality. **Fix:** < and > mean the boundary itself does not count, and a dashed line is how you say so.

## Where it shows up

- **Briefing** — safe-altitude zones against distance from the field.
- **Sortie HUD** — no-fly regions on the tactical map.
- **Systems:** Avionics.
- **Real aircraft:** a minimum safe altitude chart is a shaded region, and the boundary line is the altitude you must stay above.
