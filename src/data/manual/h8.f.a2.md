## What it is

The same linear function can arrive as a table, a graph, an equation or a sentence. Pull the same two numbers out of each and they can all be compared.

**Key idea: Find the RATE OF CHANGE and the INITIAL VALUE in each representation, then compare those two numbers.**

## How to solve it

1. Find the RATE OF CHANGE in each: how much y moves for each 1 of x. In an equation it is the number next to x.
2. From a table, divide the change in y by the change in x. The change in y on its own is not the rate unless x steps by 1.
3. Find the INITIAL value in each: the y when x is 0. In an equation it is the number on its own.
4. Now compare like with like. "Faster" compares the rates; "starts higher" compares the initial values.

## Watch out for

- **Mistake:** comparing intercepts when the question asked about rates. **Fix:** underline which one is being asked for. Starting higher and changing faster are different things.
- **Mistake:** reading the change in y off a table as if it were the rate. **Fix:** divide by the change in x. If x jumps by 4, the rate is a quarter of what the y column moved.

## Where it shows up

- **Briefing** — two fuel plans, one as a table and one in words.
- **Debrief** — comparing two aircraft's climb profiles.
- **Systems:** Fuel, Engines.
- **Real aircraft:** two aircraft's performance charts almost never come in the same format, which is why this skill exists.
