## What it is

Draw a square on each side of a RIGHT triangle and the two small squares together cover exactly the big one. That picture is the Pythagorean theorem.

**Key idea: In a right triangle, a² + b² = c², with c the side opposite the right angle. Read it backwards too: if a² + b² = c², the triangle IS right-angled.**

## How to solve it

1. Find the right angle. The side OPPOSITE it is the hypotenuse, and it is always the longest of the three.
2. The other two sides are the legs. Square each: that is the area of the square drawn on that side.
3. The two leg squares ADD UP to the square on the hypotenuse. That is what the picture is showing.
4. Read it backwards: if a² + b² comes to c², the triangle is right-angled. If it does not, it is not.

## Watch out for

- **Mistake:** putting a leg where c belongs. **Fix:** c is always the longest side and always opposite the right angle. Sort the three lengths first.
- **Mistake:** adding the sides without squaring, so 3 + 4 = 5 looks like a proof. **Fix:** that is a coincidence of 3-4-5. Try 5-12-13: 5 + 12 is 17, not 13. The SQUARES are what add.

## Where it shows up

- **Briefing** — the climb triangle: ground distance, altitude gained, flight path.
- **Sortie** — the direct run against two legs flown.
- **Systems:** Avionics, Engines.
- **Real aircraft:** a climb gradient chart is a right triangle, with the flight path as the hypotenuse.
