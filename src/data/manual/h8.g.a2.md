## What it is

When a line crosses two PARALLEL lines it makes eight angles — but only two different sizes. Knowing which pairs match tells you all eight from any one of them.

**Key idea: Corresponding angles are equal. Alternate interior and alternate exterior angles are equal. Same-side interior angles add to 180.**

## How to solve it

1. Find the two crossings. Every angle at the first has a twin at the second, in the same position.
2. CORRESPONDING, ALTERNATE INTERIOR and ALTERNATE EXTERIOR pairs are EQUAL. So are vertical angles at a single crossing.
3. SAME-SIDE INTERIOR angles — both between the lines, both on the same side of the crossing line — add to 180.
4. Name the pair first, then write the equation it gives you: equal, or adding to 180.

## Watch out for

- **Mistake:** treating every pair as equal. **Fix:** same-side interior angles are the exception. If both angles sit between the parallel lines on the SAME side, they add to 180.
- **Mistake:** confusing alternate with corresponding. **Fix:** alternate means opposite sides of the crossing line. Corresponding means the same position at each crossing, like two upper-left corners.

## Where it shows up

- **Briefing** — parallel runways crossed by a taxiway.
- **Sortie** — formation lines cut by a crossing element.
- **Systems:** Avionics.
- **Real aircraft:** parallel runways are numbered the same with L and R, because a line crossing them makes the same angle with each.
