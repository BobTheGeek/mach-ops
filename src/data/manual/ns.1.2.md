## What it is

An **integer** is a whole number that can be positive, negative, or zero. Adding integers is how you total up gains and losses in one go: a climb and a descent, fuel added and fuel burned, credits earned and credits spent.

**Key idea: same signs, add the sizes and keep the sign. Different signs, subtract the sizes and keep the sign of the bigger one. A number plus its opposite is 0.**

## How to solve it

1. Write the addition with every sign shown, each number in its own brackets. That way a minus sign can never be mistaken for a subtraction.
2. Check the signs. If they match, add the sizes and keep that sign. If they differ, subtract the smaller size from the bigger one and keep the sign of the bigger one. If you spot a number and its **opposite**, they make a **zero pair** and cancel to 0.
3. Add left to right, one step at a time. Write down the running total after each step instead of trying to hold it in your head.
4. Check on the number line. A positive moves you right, a negative moves you left. Did you land where you expected?

## Watch out for

- **Mistake:** adding the sizes no matter what the signs are, so −7 + 3 comes out as −10. **Fix:** different signs means subtract. 7 − 3 = 4, and 7 is the bigger size and is negative, so the answer is −4.
- **Mistake:** keeping the sign of the first number out of habit. **Fix:** the sign comes from whichever size is bigger, not from whichever number you wrote first. 3 + (−7) is negative; −3 + 7 is positive.
- **Mistake:** writing the right size but losing the sign along the way. **Fix:** a total that is below zero must be written with its minus sign. Read your answer back against the number line before you commit it.

## Where it shows up

- **Intercept sorties** — totalling several climb and descent legs into one net change.
- **Refuel and escort** — fuel taken on from the tanker against fuel burned on the leg.
- **Systems:** Avionics (altitude), Fuel, Credits ledger.
- **Real aircraft:** the T-38 Talon climbs at roughly 33,600 ft per minute at sea level. A 1-minute climb then a 2-minute descent at 12,000 ft per minute nets out as 33,600 + (−24,000) = +9,600 ft.
