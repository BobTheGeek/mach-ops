## What it is

A **rational number** is any number you can write as a fraction of two whole numbers: 7, −13, 2.5 and −3/4 are all rational. Every one of them has a place on the number line, and that place is what lets you compare them.

**Key idea: farther right on the number line is greater. |x| is the distance from 0 and is never negative.**

## How to solve it

1. Put every value on one number line. Zero sits in the middle, negatives to the left, positives to the right. Write fractions and decimals between the whole numbers they fall between.
2. Read what the line tells you. Farther right is greater. For two negatives, the one with the bigger size is farther **left**, so it is smaller. For **absolute value**, ignore direction and read only how far the value sits from zero.
3. Name the answer and check its sign. A greater-than answer keeps the sign it had. An absolute value or a distance is never negative.

## Watch out for

- **Mistake:** saying −8 is greater than −3 because 8 is bigger than 3. **Fix:** with negatives the bigger size sits farther left. −8 is left of −3, so −8 is the smaller one.
- **Mistake:** thinking absolute value flips the sign of every number, so |5| becomes −5. **Fix:** absolute value is a distance. It strips the sign off; it never adds one. |5| = 5 and |−5| = 5.
- **Mistake:** deciding −1/2 is greater than −1/4 because 2 is smaller than 4. **Fix:** a bigger denominator means smaller pieces. 1/2 is bigger than 1/4, so −1/2 sits farther left and is the smaller number.

## Where it shows up

- **Intercept sorties** — sorting contacts by altitude when some are below your own.
- **Refuel and escort** — reading fuel offsets above and below reserve.
- **Systems:** Avionics (altitude tape), Fuel.
- **Real aircraft:** the SR-71 cruised near 85,000 ft while outside air ran about −56 °C. The altitude is far above zero and the temperature far below it, and absolute value is how you say how far each one is from its zero point.
