## What it is

Two equations, two lines. The solution to the pair is the ONE point that sits on both of them, which on a graph is exactly where they cross.

**Key idea: The solution is the point where the two lines cross. Graph both, read the crossing, then check it in BOTH equations.**

## How to solve it

1. Graph both lines. Each one starts at its b on the y-axis and then climbs or falls by its m.
2. The solution is the one point that is on both lines: where they cross.
3. Read BOTH coordinates. An x on its own is not a solution to a pair of equations.
4. Check it by putting both numbers back into both equations. If it works in only one, it is on only one line.

## Watch out for

- **Mistake:** answering with just the x value. **Fix:** a point needs two numbers. The answer is (3, −2), not 3.
- **Mistake:** reading off a y-intercept instead of the crossing. **Fix:** the intercept is where ONE line meets the y-axis. The solution is where the TWO lines meet each other.

## Where it shows up

- **Briefing** — where two aircraft on different headings will meet.
- **Sortie HUD** — an intercept point on the tactical grid.
- **Systems:** Avionics.
- **Real aircraft:** a collision-avoidance system solves exactly this, with two tracks as two lines.
