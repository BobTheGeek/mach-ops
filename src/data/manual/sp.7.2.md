## What it is

Theoretical probability is what SHOULD happen, worked out from the setup. Experimental probability is what DID happen, counted from a log of real trials. They are rarely equal, and the gap between them is not a fault.

**Key idea: Theoretical = favourable ÷ total. Experimental = occurrences ÷ trials. A prediction is P × the number of trials. More trials brings the two closer.**

## How to solve it

1. Decide which one the question wants. "Should" means theoretical; a table of what happened means experimental.
2. Write it as a count over a count: favourable over total, or occurrences over trials.
3. To PREDICT a number of events, multiply the probability by the number of trials. The probability on its own is not a prediction.
4. Expect a gap between the two. A short run wanders; a long run settles down. Only a gap far too big for chance suggests something is wrong.

## Watch out for

- **Mistake:** expecting the experiment to land exactly on the theory, and calling the die unfair when it does not. **Fix:** 1 in 6 over 60 rolls means about 10 sixes, not exactly 10. Eleven is normal.
- **Mistake:** flipping the ratio, so 12 duds in 200 launches becomes 200/12. **Fix:** occurrences on top, trials underneath. The answer must be between 0 and 1.
- **Mistake:** answering with the probability when a COUNT was asked for. **Fix:** "how many should you expect" needs P × n. 1/8 of 200 is 25, not 1/8.

## Where it shows up

- **Debrief** — expected duds against the duds you actually got.
- **Briefing** — radar hit logs across a squadron.
- **Systems:** Weapons, Avionics.
- **Real aircraft:** ejection seat test data is exactly this: a theoretical reliability figure against what the test rig actually recorded.
