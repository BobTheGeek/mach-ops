## What it is

A letter in an expression stands for a number you do not know yet. **Like terms** are the parts that carry the same letter: 3x and 5x are like terms, but 3x and 5 are not, because one counts x's and the other counts plain numbers.

**Key idea: like terms have the same variable part. Add their coefficients; the variable stays the same. 3x + 2 is not 5x.**

## How to solve it

1. Find the like terms. Anything with the same letter goes in one group, and the plain numbers go in another. They never mix.
2. Add the coefficients of the letter terms. The coefficient is the number in front; the letter itself does not change. A bare x means 1x, not nothing.
3. Add the plain numbers separately.
4. Write the two parts together, letter part first.

To **evaluate** an expression, put the given number in wherever the letter appears — in brackets, so its sign travels with it — then do the arithmetic.

## Watch out for

- **Mistake:** folding a plain number into the letter, so 3x + 2 comes out as 5x. **Fix:** 3x means three of something unknown, and 2 means two. You cannot add three missiles to two litres. They stay apart.
- **Mistake:** dropping a minus sign off one of the terms. **Fix:** the sign in front of a term belongs to it. Write it down before you start adding, not after.
- **Mistake:** treating a bare x as though it were not there. **Fix:** x on its own is 1x. It counts.

## Where it shows up

- **Briefing** — loadout weight written as an expression before the numbers are known.
- **Intercept sorties** — thrust and fuel totals that depend on a setting you choose.
- **Systems:** Engines, Fuel, Weapons.
- **Real aircraft:** the T-38 carries two crew. Writing the weight as 2c + f, where c is crew weight and f is fuel, lets you work out the total for any crew.
